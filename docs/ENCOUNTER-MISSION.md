# Encounter core (M1 and M4)

State: source implementation on `encounter-core`; final gates, council, rebase and merge evidence belong in the PR record. This mission changes only gradual learning. It adds no telemetry, runtime dependency, model service access or learning-log format change.

## Rules and parameters

1. **Encounter ranking.** A learning card is under-exposed when its aggregate has fewer than eight distinct local days and its introduction (or first recorded exposure when an older card lacks an introduction day) was 0–20 calendar days ago. Due cards rank first, recently difficult cards second, under-exposed cards third, other active cards fourth and new cards fifth. A new Spanish word with the same first four accent-folded letters and at least 70% length overlap between its English anchor and target form ranks before other new words. Articles are ignored. This is a cue, not proof of cognacy; the model and deterministic sense validator still decide whether a swap fits.
2. **Daily placement cap.** Three `seen` events for a learning card in one browser-local calendar day exclude it from that request's candidate list. The final delivery path checks again after model/cache awaits. Per-card `seenDay` and saturated `seenCount` are scoped with the card. They count actual page sightings reported through the existing worker event path, rather than model proposals. A clock rollback cannot mint another day. Concurrent in-flight deliveries before their page sightings arrive may overshoot; the cap is enforced against acknowledged sightings, not unacknowledged proposals.
3. **Peek rule.** Two `peek` events for a rated learning card in one local day set an effective due time to the earlier of the FSRS due and `now + 24 hours`. This one-day shortening never pushes a review later. The original FSRS state and due remain stored; `peekDue` is a bounded earlier override, cleared by the next rating. Subsequent peeks can only bring the override earlier. New, maintenance and claimed-known cards are unaffected. The rule uses `peekDay` and saturated `peekCount`, not the lifetime log tuple.
4. **Maintenance and claims.** In gradual mode, a third spaced Easy rating marks maintenance but keeps the FSRS schedule and due queue. A pre-existing `familiar` card migrates on read to `maintenance:true` without changing its due, interval, review count, FSRS state, introduction or easy streak. Re-reading is idempotent. Its old due can be past: the CTO chose to preserve that one-time backlog. Learning-due cards precede maintenance-due cards across pack and personal scopes, and maintenance never contributes to the introduction backlog throttle. A `retiredReturn:true` marker on old familiar cards tracks exactly how many are currently due from retirement. The first graded retrieval changes it to `false`; that completion tombstone survives gradual-off writes and prevents a second migration count when gradual returns. Review and My words show "N words returned from retirement to be verified" while the count is positive. `Already know` records `claimAt` and gates the next retrieval until at least 21 days later or the existing FSRS due, whichever is later. The claim itself is not a retrieval and does not alter FSRS memory. A later pass at a gap of at least 21 days verifies it in `progress.js`; Again removes the claim and maintenance status. Both the review page and My words use this rule. With gradual off, legacy familiar behavior remains.
5. **Coverage.** The worker counts running words only in the passages of the existing request. Its request-wide budget for due, active and new swapped placements is `max(1, round(0.02 × running words))`. The one-slot floor keeps a short near-viewport request useful, so a short request can exceed 2%. Maintenance/known filler does not consume this unknown-word budget. The prompt states the budget, and a deterministic post-validation filter enforces it across cached and fresh passages regardless of density. If several unknown swaps fit, candidate priority decides which survive. No other page text is collected.
6. **Polish-1 integration.** After PR #36 merged first, the read-only `nextDue` accessor uses `dueAt(card)`: maintenance cards with a future effective due are included; legacy retired cards are excluded; peek acceleration and claim gates decide the displayed date. `reviewRate` returns a read-only `returnable` flag from the same predicate so an Easy maintenance rating remains in the older-worker session fallback. Version 0.12.17 is the next patch after PR #36's 0.12.16.

## Storage and compatibility

The exposure aggregate remains `[lifetime seen, lifetime peeks, distinct days, first day, last day]`. It has no daily count. The CTO approved a scoped bend: per-card `seenDay`, `seenCount`, `peekDay`, `peekCount`, `peekDue`, `maintenance`, `retiredReturn`, `claimAt` and `claimDue` are additive review-state fields. For `retiredReturn`, absent means no migration record, `true` awaits its first graded return, and `false` records completion. Both boolean values survive gradual-off writes; the off-mode card omits `maintenance`, so the return count stays zero. Schema-2 backup accepts a pending `true` on a familiar card with or without `maintenance`, as well as a completed `false`; schema 1 output omits the field. Existing familiar cards retain their original fields and gain `maintenance` and `retiredReturn` when read in gradual mode. `familiar` stays as the compatibility marker. No log write path or format changes. A schema-1 export cannot preserve the new maintenance and daily fields, as it cannot preserve FSRS state; use schema 2 for current installs.

The CTO extended repair authority to the My words claim and Practice now branches, its row builders and Reset stale-version guard; the latter compares `claimAt`/`claimDue` as well as the prior version fields. `personal.js` and `worker-context.js` pass the gradual flag so persisted personal `peekDue` survives context reads. Narrow review-status, review-page, progress and words-page display edits show the migration count and maintenance due dates. No router or log write changed.

## Evidence and limits

Focused Bun tests pin the ranking order and 21-day/eight-day boundaries, cognate ordering, the request cap across passages, worker event counters, gradual-off behavior, old-card migration and idempotence, two local time zones, claim verification, My words actions and both backup schemas. The full Bun, Python, Chromium and packaging gates are recorded with the final SHA in the PR comment. Chromium uses synthetic Gemini; no live Gemini result or learning effect is claimed.

`progress.js` provides measurements that can challenge these rules after enough local use: `exposure.atTarget` and `medianDays` against `nextDayRecall` for the encounter target; `longRetention.rate` and `verifiedKnown` for maintenance and claims; `today.peeked`/`today.flipped` together with `nextDayRecall` for the coverage cap. A causal test requires cohorts or a controlled cap comparison, which the current local summary does not provide. The existing `learning-log.js` `peekRate` and next-review outcome can challenge the peek rule, but `progress.js` does not yet expose a per-word peek-to-recall comparison; no effect can be inferred from this release alone. The research thresholds are hypotheses, not measured improvements.
