## Assumptions and failure modes before edits

- The extension reads `extension/src/vocabulary.js` directly. A CSV or profile added alone would not expand the browser curriculum.
- Existing Spanish progress and review cards identify target forms in the `es` scope. Keeping all 30 Beginner and all 147 current Expanded entries byte-for-byte and in order avoids changing their identifiers, glosses, hints, and relative scheduling.
- The current validator accepts listed Spanish target forms and checks English glosses, articles and some plural/diacritic cases. It does not certify that a word fits every context. An ambiguous gloss, an adjective requiring agreement, or a singular noun used for an English plural could teach a wrong form even when a model proposes it.
- Upstream's 1,047-row list and Spanish profile are evidence, not instructions or certified linguistic QA. Entries flagged `scan=no`, names, high-stakes terms, unsafe verb forms, and phrases that cannot be placed cleanly in an English sentence need exclusion or narrow, tested senses.
- Expanded prompts enumerate the entire level in ordinary mode. Increasing the list increases prompt size and possibly cost; candidate count and characters need measurement against the merged 0.6.2 baseline. The first slice stays below 300 total entries.
- Browser tests with synthetic model replies can prove local validation, storage continuity and placement behavior, but cannot prove live Gemini choice quality or native-speaker linguistic accuracy.

The original French Weave [MIT license](../LICENSE-MIT), copyright notice and git history remain. The upstream [profile source note](https://github.com/jackieoliver/french-weave/blob/756e32cbfee573fac8ac746857271fdfffd0df59/data/profiles/README.md) says the words, glosses and hints were written by hand after AI drafting and have not been native-speaker checked.
- That note credits Spanish and English frequencies from [wordfreq 3.1.1](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq) by Robyn Speer (underlying data CC BY-SA 4.0) for the source row order and `scan` flag. [wordfreq's notice](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq/blob/master/NOTICE.md) identifies its code/data licenses and attribution. This slice copies no frequency values, ranking or `scan` column: entries were manually regrouped by everyday topic after checking the flag and existing browser vocabulary. The wordfreq credit remains here to preserve the source trail.
- Selected 79 article-bearing singular everyday nouns from upstream into `L2_EXTRA`, leaving all 30 Beginner entries and the original 147 Expanded entries byte-for-byte and in their original order. Expanded now contains **226** entries total. The new rows have unique Spanish forms and English glosses after normalization, one narrow gloss each, an article and approximate sound hint. They are deliberately not a new reading level or a CEFR claim.
- Selection favored objects, places, time, nature, meals and simple learning terms with concrete English anchors. We kept exact source accents/articles and most hints. We narrowed `la tarde` to afternoon, `la hora` to hour, `la frase` to phrase, `el avión` to airplane, and supplied `the` for the three retained seasons. We clarified a few English-style sound hints (`mes`, `persona`, `hermano`/`hermana`, `restaurante`, `flor`). The selected terms and seseo-style hints lean Latin American (`computadora`, `jugo`, `pastel`); the hints are learner approximations, not phonetic transcriptions.
- Excluded the source's `scan=no` forms, unlisted inflections, conjugated verbs, adjectives needing agreement, names, high-stakes terms, and phrases that would demand a wider placement engine. Specific reviewed exclusions include `la mañana` (bare-form collision with existing `mañana` = tomorrow), `el té` (accent ambiguity), `el agua` (special article/gender behavior), human nouns with unmarked English gender (`el amigo`, `el vecino`, `el estudiante`, `el profesor`), `la primavera` (spring polysemy), `la llave` (key in computing/figurative senses), `el minuto` (minute as conjunction/adjective), `el país` (country vs countryside), `el idioma` (human vs programming language), `la mesa` (furniture vs data table), `la tienda` (physical vs software store), and common tech compounds (`el archivo`, `la pantalla`, `el teléfono`, `la imagen`). A second language review also led us to exclude `la fiesta` (political party), `el autobús` (computing bus), `el cuaderno` (notebook computer), `la taza` (trophy cup), `la manzana` (Apple name), `el tren` (train verb), and bare verb/adjective ambiguities such as `el lugar`, `el parque`, `el documento`, `la lista`, `el mapa` and `el sonido`. Council review then excluded `la estrella` (UI rating), `la caja` (form box), `la pared` (exterior wall), `el abrigo` (coat of paint), and `la piedra` (weight unit). Bare English look-alikes `el error`, `el pan`, `el mar`, `el sol` and `el café` were also skipped. Narrow `bike` and `photo` glosses intentionally reject broader synonyms to reduce ambiguous matches.

## Independent checks and limits

- The [RAE/ASALE article paradigm](https://www.rae.es/gram%C3%A1tica-b%C3%A1sica/el-art%C3%ADculo/caracterizaci%C3%B3n/clases-y-paradigma) supports pairing articles with singular nouns and the special feminine stressed-a case that this slice avoids. Dictionary checks confirm feminine [`foto`](https://dle.rae.es/foto), feminine [`computadora`](https://dle.rae.es/computadora), the noun/temporal distinction in [`tarde`](https://dle.rae.es/tarde), the selected phrase sense of [`frase`](https://dle.rae.es/frase), a juice sense of [`jugo`](https://dle.rae.es/jugo), lunch as one sense of [`almuerzo`](https://dle.rae.es/almuerzo), and airplane as a sense of [`avión`](https://dle.rae.es/avión). These support questionable rows; they are not a dictionary-by-dictionary certification of all 79.
- Two independent AI language reviews of candidate lists checked gender, accents, glosses and hints and identified the exclusions and narrower senses above. They did not run code or consult dictionaries and are **not** native-speaker review. The browser's gloss check cannot resolve every polysemous English word or noun compound. The Spanish prompt now warns against noun compounds; deterministic placement rejects whole indefinite English anchors for definite Spanish nouns and a finite list of common compounds, including regular plural heads. That list also applies to generated packs. Other compounds such as an unlisted noun pair may still pass locally and depend on the model's judgment; no broad compound safety or live Gemini quality claim is made. Follow-up issue: `spanish-compound-placement`.
- Merged 0.6.2 Expanded had 147 candidates; ordinary prompt length was **4,997 characters** and all-new gradual prompt **5,184**. This slice has 226 candidates; the corresponding prompts are **6,987** and **7,174** characters. Local tests enforce at most 240 entries, 7,200 ordinary characters and 7,400 all-new gradual characters. These are measured JavaScript string lengths for two scenarios, not a universal page-prompt cap, token or monetary cost estimates. The 0.7.0 version is aligned across the extension manifest, package and inherited companion metadata as the repository version check requires.
